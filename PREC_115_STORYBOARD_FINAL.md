### 1. The Issue and Why It Is Important

Our project investigates the dangerous convergence of subscription capitalism, digital feudalism, and brain-computer interfaces (BCIs). In the contemporary digital economy, corporate monopolies have systematically dismantled the concept of consumer property ownership. Consumers no longer buy products; they buy conditional, temporary access governed by End User License Agreements (EULAs). Automakers like BMW charge recurring monthly fees to activate heated seats physically manufactured into vehicles, software corporations lock essential creative tools behind cloud paywalls, and agricultural giants like John Deere use digital rights management (DRM) to prevent farmers from repairing their own tractors. Cory Doctorow terms this ongoing corporate capture "enshittification": platforms lock in users, degrade service quality, and extract continuous economic rents.

CortechOS takes this economic model and extrapolates it into the near future of neurotechnology. As neural interface firms like Neuralink, Synchron, and Blackrock Neurotech transition from experimental medical prosthetics toward commercial consumer augmentation, tech monopolies will inevitably apply the Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) business model to human biology itself. 

This issue is of paramount importance because it threatens fundamental human agency and biological autonomy. When access to focus, memory recall, sleep, and emotional regulation is converted into a monthly recurring subscription, the human mind ceases to be an autonomous sanctuary. It becomes proprietary hardware leased from a corporation. If your credit card fails, your basic cognitive functions are subject to artificial throttling, surveillance, and corporate repossession.

### 2. Description and Justification of the Medium

To explore this concept, we designed and built a two-part multimedia ecosystem:
1. **A live, publicly deployed corporate web platform** engineered in HTML5, Tailwind CSS, and JavaScript, hosted at [https://prasiddhadshrestha.github.io/cortech-os/](https://prasiddhadshrestha.github.io/cortech-os/).
2. **A polished, 90-second satirical broadcast commercial** featuring customer testimonials, an overly enthusiastic infomercial host, and a rapid pharmaceutical-style legal disclaimer.

This multimedia combination is uniquely appropriate for our thesis. Predatory subscription capitalism does not present itself as a grim, dystopian boot stamping on a face; it presents itself through clean Nordic typography, cheerful customer testimonials, polished corporate UI, and optimistic Silicon Valley marketing. 

An academic essay can describe corporate manipulation in the abstract, but constructing the corporation's actual customer-facing apparatus forces the audience to experience the cognitive dissonance firsthand. By allowing the viewer to interact with the live pricing matrix, slide the interactive neural resolution tool (watching human memory degrade from 4K HDR down to a pixelated 360p blur), and experience the coercive $5,000 cancellation modal, the medium itself becomes the argument. The form is not decorative; it is the satirical engine that exposes corporate ideology.

### 3. Why the Project Succeeds

The project succeeds by constructing a coherent, self-reinforcing institutional satire rather than a collection of disconnected jokes. The exploitation operates on a recognizable five-stage corporate loop:
* **Stage 1 (Manufactured Dependency):** The corporation frames natural human biology as defective technical debt, marketing cognitive implants as essential for workplace survival.
* **Stage 2 (Artificial Tiering):** Basic human needs are artificially segmented. Standard ($0/mo) caps sleep at 5 hours and injects unskippable audio ads every ten minutes; Gold ($4,999/mo) compresses eight hours of sleep into three; Platinum ($14,999/mo) uninstalls sleep completely for 120-hour consecutive work weeks.
* **Stage 3 (Monetized Deprivation):** Users on lower tiers are deliberately throttled during peak afternoon hours, forcing them to pay to recover natural biological clarity.
* **Stage 4 (Preventing Exit):** When Michael attempts to cancel, he discovers that returning to his original biological brain requires a non-negotiable $5,000 exit fee.
* **Stage 5 (Normalization):** The manic Platinum customer (Ryan) celebrates that his emotional grief was chemically deleted when his dog passed away so he could finish three corporate spreadsheets twenty minutes later.

The project succeeds because the corporation never admits to being evil. It frames extreme human alienation as personal optimization, career empowerment, and modern convenience. Viewers laugh at the initial absurdity, only to recognize that every single mechanism depicted already exists in modern consumer technology.

### 4. What We Want the Audience to Take Away

We want the audience to reject **technological determinism**: the false ideology that technological outcomes are an inevitable, unchangeable force of nature that society must passively accept. Grounded in Cory Doctorow's critical framework in *Tied to the Tracks: What Is Science Fiction For?*, science fiction is not an exercise in gadget prediction; it is an adversarial thought experiment that reveals who benefits from a technological system and who gets crushed beneath it.

The audience must recognize that the primary danger of future neurotechnology is not the physical microchip. Neural interfaces possess miraculous potential to cure neurological paralysis and restore lost human senses. The true danger is the extractive economic system governing the hardware. When essential biological infrastructure is monopolized by private corporations, human freedom is reduced to a revocable lease.

Ultimately, we want the audience to confront the reality of structural coercion. The defining question of future technology is not whether an individual would voluntarily choose to install a brain implant today. The true question is: when your employer, your coworkers, and your competitors upgrade to executive tiers, how long will economic pressure allow you to keep your natural, unaugmented biological brain?
