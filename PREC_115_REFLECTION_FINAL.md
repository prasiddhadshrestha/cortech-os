### 2. Project Success and Academic Integration (Cory Doctorow & Course Themes)

The ultimate success of CortechOS lies in how effectively it converts abstract critical theory into a tangible, experiential simulation. Our primary theoretical anchor throughout this project was Cory Doctorow's essay, *Tied to the Tracks: What Is Science Fiction For?*

Doctorow forcefully dismantles the myth of **technological determinism**: the fatalistic belief that technological evolution is an autonomous, inevitable force that society must simply accept. Tech monopolies routinely weaponize determinism to stifle regulation, telling the public that surveillance, planned obsolescence, and subscription locks are merely the natural cost of modern progress. Doctorow counters that technologies are always the product of human decisions, corporate incentives, and power struggles. Science fiction does not exist to predict flashy gadgets; its highest purpose is adversarial modeling: asking who gets tied to the tracks when the railroad gets built.

CortechOS brings Doctorow's railroad metaphor into direct confrontation with human biology:
* **The Locomotive:** The executives and ultra-wealthy subscribers who afford the Platinum tier ($14,999/month). They leverage unlimited cognitive overclocking, sleep elimination, and artificial emotional suppression to monopolize economic productivity.
* **Tied to the Tracks:** The working-class and entry-level employees on the Standard tier ($0/month). Their cognitive faculties are artificially throttled during peak work hours, their subconscious minds are flooded with invasive pop-up audio ads, and their basic biological recovery is criminalized as lost workplace efficiency.

This dynamic illustrates Doctorow's concept of **enshittification** alongside modern digital feudalism. In contemporary capitalism, companies like Apple, John Deere, and BMW lock consumers into closed hardware silos, stripping away the Right to Repair and replacing genuine ownership with revocable licenses. CortechOS asks: what happens when that exact economic extraction is installed beneath your skull?

To prove that our satirical premise is not far-fetched speculation, we grounded our research in real-world precedent: the 2020 collapse of **Second Sight Medical Products**. Second Sight manufactured the Argus II retinal prosthesis, an FDA-approved bionic eye implant. When the company encountered financial distress and pivoted business models, it abruptly ceased technical support for the hardware. Over 350 blind patients worldwide were left stranded with obsolete, unsupported microchips and electrode arrays physically screwed into their eyes and brains. Patients reported system crashes, sudden blindness, and medical terror, with no replacement parts or software patches available. Second Sight proved that corporate medical abandonment is already an empirical reality. CortechOS merely demonstrates the next logical progression when health infrastructure is subjugated to quarterly subscription margins.

### 3. Differences Between the Initial Storyboard and the Final Build

The final version of CortechOS evolved substantially from our initial conceptual outline. What began as a conventional, single-medium video project matured into an immersive, multi-platform media ecosystem.

The key differences between our initial storyboard and final build include:

1. **Evolution from Single Video to Multi-Platform Ecosystem:**
   * *Initial Storyboard:* We originally planned to produce a straightforward 60-to-90 second satirical video sketch uploaded to YouTube or submitted as a standalone video file.
   * *Final Build:* We realized that a passive video could only tell the audience about exploitation, rather than letting them experience it. We designed, coded, and deployed a fully functional corporate website ([https://prasiddhadshrestha.github.io/cortech-os/](https://prasiddhadshrestha.github.io/cortech-os/)). This enabled the audience to interact with the pricing matrix, test the memory resolution degradation slider, and trigger the coercive cancellation flow in real time.

2. **Radical Escalation of the Pricing Architecture:**
   * *Initial Storyboard:* Our early draft featured modest consumer pricing tiers ($29/month for Basic, $79/month for Pro), modeled after streaming services like Netflix or Adobe Creative Cloud.
   * *Final Build:* We realized modest consumer pricing failed to communicate the extreme class violence inherent in cognitive enhancement. We redesigned the tiering to reflect Silicon Valley enterprise SaaS contracts: Free ($0/month with severe throttling and audio ads), Gold ($4,999/month), and Platinum ($14,999/month). This radical polarization made the dystopian class divide immediate: under unregulated capitalism, cognitive supremacy belongs exclusively to corporate capital.

3. **Invention of the Coercive Cancellation Trap:**
   * *Initial Storyboard:* The original storyboard focused primarily on the daily annoyances of using the device (ads playing in one's head).
   * *Final Build:* We added an explicit exit barrier: the $5,000 cancellation fee to disconnect from CortechOS and regain natural biological brain function. This structural trap mirrored real-world telecom lock-ins, proprietary medical ecosystems, and health insurance penalties, demonstrating that once biological infrastructure is privatized, freedom becomes financially unaffordable.

4. **Integration of the 3D Hardware Reveal:**
   * *Initial Storyboard:* Hardware was purely conceptual and referred to only through dialogue.
   * *Final Build:* We generated a custom 3D animation featuring a rotating chrome dinosaur microchip on a holographic pedestal. Grounding the intangible software in physical bio-surveillance hardware gave the fictional corporation immediate industrial credibility.

5. **Live Presentation Strategy (The Cold Open):**
   * *Initial Storyboard:* A traditional presentation format: introducing the topic with introductory slides, explaining the course themes, and then screening the video.
   * *Final Build:* We executed a theatrical Cold Open. We launched straight into the commercial without introductory commentary, allowing the audience to absorb the polished corporate pitch on its own terms. Only after the room experienced the satirical tension did we deconstruct the underlying economic engine through the live website demo and Doctorow's adversarial sci-fi framework.
